Agents write a 100-word monologue for a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired, and a judge model playing a veteran game writer compares them two at a time.

This entry's monologue: a dark lord receives a challenger in his hall and cannot be bothered to rise. The exhaustion lives in the voice, the pauses and the small deflections, never in a plain statement of weariness. He is still a villain, with a scheme (the eastern provinces by Tuesday, a treaty with the salt barons) and a history (forty years of rising for kings, priests and his own father), and the sting lands in the last line, where the hero's death is discussed like a piece of paperwork, slowly, in no particular hurry.

The entry text (100 words, 527 characters):

> Yes, yes, the banners, the oath, the kneeling. I have heard it all before, from better men, in colder halls. The eastern provinces are mine by Tuesday, or so my generals insist, and the treaty with the salt barons waits on my desk, unsigned, beside a cup gone cold. Forgive me if I do not rise to meet your sword. I have risen for forty years. I have risen for kings, for priests, for my own father. Sit. The fire is warm, the wine is poured, and we can discuss your death like civilized people, slowly, in no particular hurry.
